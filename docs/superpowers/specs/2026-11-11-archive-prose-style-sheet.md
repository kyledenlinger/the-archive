# The Archive: Prose Style Sheet and Continuity Outline

## Patterns found

| Pattern | Example | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Abstraction in place of a picture | “This is the place where everything anyone ever made ends up.” | Say what the player sees and why they came. |
| “Not X: the Y kind of X” setups | “The Archive is quiet. Not peacefully quiet: the kind of quiet…” | State the detail once. |
| Fragment runs for atmosphere | “Small. Fast. Wearing a suit…” / “Nothing is labeled. There is no map.” / “It takes an afternoon. It sounds different. It’s yours.” | Join into ordinary sentences. One short sentence for emphasis, at most, per passage. |
| List padding | “Films, photographs, songs, letters, games, drawings on napkins.” / “posted, pinned, re-uploaded, shared ten thousand times” | Keep a list only when every item matters to the player. |
| Twisted idioms | “a suit that has seen better decades” | Use the idiom as people say it (“seen better days”) or plain words. |
| Setting contradictions | Onboarding puts you inside the Archive; stop 1 says “Deep in a video site you find…” | Everything is found *in the Archive*. Its tags record where a copy has been (“uploaded to a video site by…”). |
| Ideas before their setup | “Whether you’re allowed to take it…” before any reason to take anything; the cart’s purpose is explained last. | First passage: you’re a creator, you need material for a project, the Archive is where you look, the cart holds what you take. |
| Repetition patterns (“Looks at you. Looks at… Looks back…”) | onboarding 2 | Once. |

## Rules

1. Every passage answers, in order: where am I, what am I looking at, what do I need to decide. Mood comes from details, not from labels.
2. Plain sentences that connect (*and, but, so, when*). Vary length naturally; no strings of fragments.
3. One concrete detail beats three. Lists only when they inform.
4. Idioms as people say them, or none.
5. Legal statements keep their exact meaning. If one reads badly, rewrite the sentence without changing the claim; flag any claim that seems wrong instead of changing it.
6. Always “you,” never a group size. No verdicts. No stance on AI.
7. Text only: no changes to IDs, keys, order, or the number of entries.

## Voices

**Narrator.** A warm, plain storyteller: past the door, into the room, onto the next shelf. Present tense, second person. Short paragraphs, specific nouns, light humor that comes from the situation rather than wordplay.

**The Clerk.** A person talking: he starts sentences, backs off, apologizes, and makes the odd dry joke. Contractions, hedges (“honestly,” “I think,” “more or less”), and the occasional “That’s not advice, obviously.” No more than one signature tic per speech. His clues (no name tag; “Nobody’s ever been able to tell me”; humming a tune he can’t place; “developed once”; the quoted letter; flinching at TAKEDOWN; watching the license cards; “Could you do that for me?”) stay, but each is a small moment, not a spotlight.

**Instructional text** (debriefs, cart notes, reference, license summaries). Clear and exact, like a good teacher’s handout. Define a term the first time it matters. Cart notes are records: past tense, what was done, why it matters. No jokes.
